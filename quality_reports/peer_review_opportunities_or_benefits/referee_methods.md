# Referee B Report — methods (MEASUREMENT disposition)

**Paper:** Opportunities or Benefits: Local Conditions and Refugee Labor Market Integration
**Target journal:** Review of Economic Studies (ReStud)
**Date:** 2026-05-18
**Referee:** B (methods) | Disposition: MEASUREMENT | Paper type: reduced-form
**Critical peeve:** End-to-end sample-construction waterfall.
**Constructive peeve:** Raw-data figures before model output.

## Summary recommendation

**Major revision.** The identification logic is reasonable and the substantive question is well-motivated, but the measurement layer of this paper — sample construction, variable definitions, treatment assignment, and inference — is, in its current state, not at the level expected of a ReStud manuscript. Several issues are individually fixable, but collectively they prevent me from being confident that the reported coefficients identify what the paper claims they identify. The required revisions are substantial but tractable: a documented sample-construction waterfall, clarifying the geographic and temporal definition of IVUR and IPBL, a defense of the SE clustering choice given only 9 states, and an honest accounting of the "potential" vs. "received" benefits gap. None of these require a new dataset; they require disclosure and reframing of what is already there.

## One-paragraph summary of the paper (my read, focused on measurement choices)

The authors link Austrian ASSD administrative records (supplemented with BMAW PES files) to identify a cohort of 39,694 refugees who received Convention or subsidiary protection between Jan 2011 and Aug 2018 and who maintained insurance coverage for 60 months. They construct two "initial" treatments measured at the month and place of protection: $IVUR_{d_0,t_0}$ — a three-month-summed vacancy-to-unemployment ratio at the district of protection (defined in 3_data.tex eq. (1)); and $IPBL_{f,p,s_0,t_0}$ — a hand-coded potential benefit level (excluding housing) varying by family status × protection type × state × month (eq. (2)). Identification rests on within-arrival-group (gender × initial-state × quarter-of-arrival × alone/family) variation in placement, plus district and month-year fixed effects to address unobserved regional confounders. Outcomes (employment, marginal employment, real earnings, log wages conditional on employment, residency-in-initial-state, residency-in-Vienna, PES use, welfare receipt) are tracked monthly over 60 months. SEs are clustered at the state-year-of-protection level.

## Strengths

1. **Treatment timing is conceptually right.** Measuring the local conditions at the moment of labor-market access (protection grant) rather than at arrival is the correct conceptual move and the comparison with arrival-measured treatments in Section 5.5 (Table `rob_ivur_ipbl_arrival`) is informative — the arrival-measured IVUR/IPBL load on zero, which is a real result and the paper should make more of it.
2. **The dual exogenous source — placement quotas plus a sequence of state-level reforms (Lower Austria 2016, Upper Austria 2016, Lower Austria 2017, Burgenland 2017) — is genuine variation.** The simultaneous use of cross-sectional and within-state-over-time variation is a clear advance over single-margin studies (e.g., Ferwerda 2023).
3. **The balance regressions in Table `treatment_characteristics_corr` (Section 4.2.1) are a reasonable diagnostic** for the cell-assignment assumption, and the Wald-test framing is the right one. Including the with- and without-arrival-group columns is the right comparison.
4. **The ATT scaling argument in Section 7** (multiplying by ~3 to account for the ~35% of subsidiary-protected outside Vienna who actually live in private accommodation) is the right kind of measurement transparency and should be expanded, not buried in Discussion.

## Major concerns

### MC1: Sample-construction waterfall is absent.

- **Category:** Sample construction
- **Issue:** Section 3.1 of `3_data.tex` (lines 7–14) jumps from "ASSD = all social insurance episodes" + "BMAW supplement covers individuals granted protection and registered with PES" directly to a final number of "41,212 refugees, among whom 39,694 maintained active insurance coverage for a minimum of five years post-protection." The intermediate counts are missing. A reader cannot reconstruct:
  - The universe of asylum decisions in 2011–Aug 2018 (the paper invokes BMI/BFA statistics in cross-references in `appendix.tex` lines 354–378 — a commented-out table — but the official numbers are not in the published version);
  - The PES-registration filter (the paper acknowledges in Section 3.1 that "registration at the PES is voluntary" and that "access to social benefits linked to registration encourages it" but does not report what share of the BFA universe this filter retains);
  - How many refugees are dropped because IVUR could not be computed (footnote 4 says "a few districts" with "official zeroes in unemployment count" — how many people, which districts, which years?);
  - How many are dropped by the "protection within three years of arrival" rule;
  - How many are dropped by the "not granted on the day of arrival" rule (family reunification proxy);
  - How many fall out via the 18–60 age filter at immigration;
  - The 1,518-person gap between 41,212 and 39,694 (insurance lapse within 60 months) — this *is* sample selection on a post-treatment outcome (Section A.2 / `no_insurance` argues IVUR/IPBL do not predict insurance loss, but the magnitudes are not reported in the body).
  
  In short, the reader is asked to take a 41,212 → 39,694 chain on faith. For a ReStud paper, this is below the bar. The footnote in Appendix `appendix.tex` lines 354–378 contains the comparison to BMI statistics in a *commented-out* `table:amsbmistatus` — the *only* place in the paper where coverage rates (78% on average, 92% at the 2015–2016 peak) appear is in a hidden comment. The paper says "coverage is 67% for GC refugees and 96% for subsidiary-protected in 2015" in commented-out text at `8_conclusion.tex` line 5. This is unacceptable: the editor and the reader should see the coverage rates in the published version.

- **What would change my mind:** A Table 1 (or Appendix Table A.1) that is a literal flowchart: "BFA universe of asylum decisions 2011-08/2018: X. After dropping protection-on-day-of-arrival: Y. After 18–60 at immigration: Z. After ≤3-year asylum process: W. After PES registration link (BMAW match): V. After IVUR computable: U. After 60-month observation: 39,694." With at minimum the share of BFA universe retained at each step (using the AMS/BMI comparison from the commented-out appendix table), broken down by Convention/subsidiary and by arrival year (since 2015–2016 cohort dominates). Also bring `tab:amsbmistatus` out of comments into the appendix.

### MC2: The IVUR is defined at the district but measured at the municipality — and "open positions" is not specified.

- **Category:** Variable construction
- **Issue:** `3_data.tex` line 16 says vacancies and unemployed are "at the district level," but line 22 says they come from "aggregated monthly data on registered unemployment and vacancies at the *municipality* level from the ASSD," and equation (1) (line 24–27) uses subscript $d_0$ for district. This is inconsistent. If the raw data is municipality and you aggregate to district, say so explicitly and explain why (the paper claims political districts "roughly align with labor markets," line 29). Beyond the subscript issue:
  1. The numerator "open positions" is not defined. Is it the *stock* of open vacancies on a given reference day (which the AMS reports), the *flow* of newly opened vacancies in the month, or the *number of vacancies open at any time during the month* (a stock-flow hybrid)? Each gives a different VU interpretation. The denominator "unemployed individuals" has the same ambiguity (monthly stock vs. unique persons unemployed during month).
  2. The summation $\sum_{t=0}^{2}$ is over the protection month $t_0$ and the two following months — i.e., the IVUR is measured *partly post-treatment*, since refugees are free to move from month 0. If a refugee leaves district $d_0$ in month 1, the vacancies and unemployment in $d_0$ during months 1 and 2 are no longer the labor market she faces. This is not technically a violation of "initial" since $d_0$ is fixed, but it does mean IVUR mixes the initial month's tightness with two months of post-protection tightness in a place the refugee may not still live. Why not $t \in \{-2, -1, 0\}$ or $t \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$?
  3. The map in Figure 2 (`fig:ivurmap`) is an *average* over 2011–2018. It conveys cross-sectional variation but conceals the temporal variation that the district-FE specification actually exploits. Where is the figure showing the *residual* variation in IVUR after sweeping out district + month-year FE? That is the variation that identifies $\beta_1$.
  4. The paper never tells me the distribution of IVUR. The Table 1 SD is 0.15 and the IQR is 0.21 (Section 5 line 8), which together imply a heavily right-skewed distribution. Are there districts with IVUR > 1? > 2? How thin is the right tail?
  5. There are 94 political districts in Austria, with very wide population variation (Vienna is one district with 1.9M people; some Carinthian/Styrian districts have <30,000). Thin districts will have very noisy IVUR (small denominators). The robustness section does not test district aggregation alternatives, despite a comment in the source (`3_data.tex` line 29: "In Appendix XX, we investigate alternative regional aggregations and find that results are highly robust. (TO BE INCLUDED)") flagging this as a known gap.

- **What would change my mind:** (i) State precisely whether vacancies and unemployment are stocks at the reference day, monthly flows, or person-month-counts, and whether the numerator and denominator are constructed consistently; reconcile the municipality-vs-district language in lines 16 and 22 of `3_data.tex`. (ii) Add a Figure 3 (raw-data figure, per my constructive peeve) showing the *full* distribution of IVUR — kernel density across the analysis sample, split by year and by district population quintile — *before* presenting any regression. (iii) Add a residual-IVUR plot after sweeping out district × month-year FE so the reader sees what variation $\beta_1$ is identified off. (iv) Run the main specification with commuting-zone–level aggregation as the unit (Austria has ~32 NUTS-3 / commuting zones) and report whether $\beta_1$ moves. (v) Justify the choice of $t \in \{0,1,2\}$ rather than $\{-2,-1,0\}$ or $\{-1,0,1\}$; ideally report a robustness column.

### MC3: IPBL is a *potential* benefit, not a *received* benefit — and the gap is large and asymmetric across the two protection groups.

- **Category:** Variable construction
- **Issue:** The IPBL is the maximum benefit a person with no income and no wealth would receive under the state's welfare rules, conditional on family status (defined narrowly via children co-insured), protection type, state, and month. Concretely:
  1. "Potential" ≠ received. For Convention refugees, IPBL is BMS-equivalent. For subsidiary-protected, IPBL is *Grundversorgung*. As the Discussion (7_discussion.tex line 12) finally admits: "Refugees living in private accommodation are directly affected by the reduction in benefit levels, as they experience immediate changes in disposable income. By contrast, individuals in organized accommodation receive almost all support in-kind. They are therefore not directly impacted by the reform." This means that for the ~65% of subsidiary-protected outside Vienna who are in organized accommodation, the IPBL change is essentially a paper change. Even worse, the authors say "we do not observe the type of accommodation at the individual level," so the treatment is fundamentally misclassified for this subgroup. The post-hoc factor-of-three scaling in Section 7 is the wrong place to surface this; it should be a first-order discussion of the measurement.
  2. Housing benefits are excluded (3_data.tex line 26: "These are the benefits provided for covering the cost of living. They exclude housing support, which is organized quite differently in different states"). But the welfare reforms in question — particularly the Upper Austria July 2016 cut from ~838 to 522 euro — collapsed BMS, including its housing share, into a flat amount; so for some reforms IPBL captures the cut, for others (where the cut hit housing specifically) it does not. Appendix B (subsec:welfare) acknowledges this generally but does not document, per reform, whether IPBL captures the full cut.
  3. The family-status definition is "child co-insured in Austria at protection date or partnership with another asylum-seeker plus a co-insured child" (3_data.tex footnote 5). This is different from the family-arrival variable used in the arrival-group fixed effects. It is also a within-sample object — a refugee whose child arrived via family reunification after the protection date will be coded as a single, which can be wrong by tens of thousands of euros over a year. The paper acknowledges this is "imperfect" (3_data.tex line 40) but does not quantify the miscoding rate.
  4. The "single-person vs. family conversion" is opaque. Figure 1 (`fig:ipbl`) shows singles, Figure A.1 (`fig:ipbl_family`) shows "families of four." But IPBL must be applied to households of varying composition. The conversion rule (per Appendix's BMS section: minimum standards differ by adult/minor/unaccompanied minor, with household-level calculation) is not made operational in the paper.

- **What would change my mind:** (i) Add a section to Appendix B that, for each reform listed in the bullet list at lines 326–331 of `appendix.tex`, tabulates: pre-reform IPBL, post-reform IPBL, pre-reform total benefits including housing (where computable), post-reform total benefits including housing, share of the published cut captured by IPBL. If IPBL captures, e.g., 80% of the Upper Austria cut and 100% of the Lower Austria 2016 cut, say so. (ii) Run the main specification with an alternative IPBL that *includes* the housing benefit lower bound where state law specifies it. (iii) The accommodation-type issue is so consequential it must be in the main text. Move the ATT-scaling argument out of Discussion and into Section 5, and ideally find a proxy for accommodation-type (e.g., whether the refugee's protection-date ZIP code matches a known organized-accommodation address) and run the analysis as a heterogeneity. (iv) Report what fraction of refugees in the sample have a family-status code that *changes* between the protection date and 12 months later (via family reunification or child birth) — this bounds the measurement error in IPBL's family margin.

### MC4: Clustering at "state-year-of-protection" is hard to defend with only 9 states; the robustness figure (`fig:errors_all`) reveals the choice is not innocuous.

- **Category:** Standard errors
- **Issue:** The paper clusters at state-year-of-protection, yielding 9 × 8 = 72 clusters at most. Two problems:
  1. IVUR varies at the *district* × month level. IPBL varies at the *state* × protection-type × family-status × month level. There is no obvious reason the cluster of residual correlation is at state-year-of-protection rather than at the level of variation of the treatments themselves. The justification at `6_robustness.tex` line 15 — "it best aligns with the level of aggregation at which our treatment is assigned and captures the relevant sources of residual correlation" — is hand-waved. For IVUR, the natural cluster is district-by-month or district-by-year; for IPBL, state-by-month. The fact that the paper has *two* treatments at different levels argues for two-way clustering, not for picking an intermediate level.
  2. Figure A.10 (`fig:errors_all`, `different_standard_errors_all.pdf`) admits that under state-level or wild bootstrap inference, the IPBL employment confidence interval *widens substantially* (lines 14–15 of `6_robustness.tex`). The paper then chooses the *narrower* CI on the grounds that "9 states is problematic" — which is exactly backwards. With 9 states, the wild bootstrap with Webb weights is the conservative, defensible inference, not the fallback. The decision to prefer state-year SEs because state-only SEs are inconveniently wide is a measurement-side red flag.
  3. The arrival-group is the de facto unit of haphazard treatment assignment (per the identification story in `4_method.tex` line 35). Should SEs not at least account for clustering at the arrival-group level? The paper does not test this.

- **What would change my mind:** Present, side-by-side in the main results figure, the point estimate with three SE choices: (a) main (state × year-of-protection), (b) two-way (district × month, state × month), (c) wild cluster bootstrap with Webb weights at the state level. Adopt the *widest* of the three as the headline CI, or — at minimum — show the headline result with wild-bootstrap inference and live with the wider intervals. If the IPBL effect at month 12 is significant under wild bootstrap with 9 states, the paper is stronger; if it is not, the paper should say so directly.

### MC5: Multiple-treatment exposure for movers is unaddressed.

- **Category:** Multiple-treatment overlap
- **Issue:** Roughly 30% of refugees relocate within 4 months of protection (paper's headline descriptive). After moving, they face a new district's IVUR (which may be very different — most relocations are *into* Vienna, which is in the bottom of the IVUR distribution per Figure 2) and a new state's IPBL. The paper's specification holds $IVUR_{d_0,t_0}$ and $IPBL_{p,f,s_0,t_0}$ fixed at the protection-date values for the entire 60-month window. This is internally consistent with the "intent-to-treat" framing, but:
  1. The interpretation of the *long-run* coefficient (months 30–60) is then "the effect of the *original* labor-market shock at the place I no longer live" — which is what the authors actually want to estimate (locked-in effects, headstart effects, etc.). Fine. But this means the long-run coefficients are not "the effect of facing IVUR=X" — they are "the effect of being assigned to a district with IVUR=X at protection, irrespective of where you went next." This needs to be said more sharply.
  2. The post-treatment endogenous-mobility margin can also wash out the effect mechanically: if refugees in low-IVUR districts move to Vienna and refugees in high-IVUR districts stay, then by month 24 both groups face similar local labor markets and the IVUR-on-employment coefficient should mechanically fade. The paper interprets the fading effect substantively ("effects fade after 30 months"), but it could equally be a mechanical consequence of differential post-treatment mobility into similar labor markets. The paper does not distinguish these two interpretations.
  3. The "joint outcomes" figure (`fig:empl_reloc`) is well-suited to disentangling this and is in the paper, but the narrative does not fully exploit it.

- **What would change my mind:** Add a complement to the main specification that conditions on *staying in the initial district* (or initial state) and re-runs the dynamics. The contrast between "ITT" (current spec) and "stayers-only" (which is post-treatment selection but informative) would isolate the mechanical-fading hypothesis from the substantive-fading hypothesis. Also, clarify in Section 4 and again in Section 5 that the long-run coefficients are *initial-condition* effects, not contemporaneous labor-market-tightness effects.

### MC6: No raw-data figures of IVUR or IPBL variation in the analysis sample before model output.

- **Category:** Specification / transparency (constructive-peeve violation)
- **Issue:** This is my constructive peeve. The paper gives one IVUR map (`fig:ivurmap`, an average) and one IPBL time-series by state (`fig:ipbl`, singles). What is missing — and what I would expect a ReStud-quality MEASUREMENT-oriented paper to show before any regression coefficient — is:
  1. A histogram / kernel density of IVUR in the analysis sample (i.e., one observation per refugee, evaluated at her $d_0, t_0$), to see the empirical distribution that drives the estimates.
  2. A histogram / kernel density of IPBL in the analysis sample, by protection type, with the reform dates marked.
  3. A scatter of within-cell residual IVUR vs. within-cell residual IPBL — to convince the reader that the two treatments are not collinear within the FE structure.
  4. A "first-stage-style" picture of IPBL over time by state, with vertical lines marking the reform dates and the share of the analysis sample in each state-quarter overlaid (to show where the identifying mass comes from).
  Without these, the reader cannot judge whether the variation actually exists in the relevant region of the joint distribution. I expect every claim in Table 4 (`tab:interactions`) to come with a picture showing me there is enough joint variation in (IVUR, IPBL) to identify the interaction.

- **What would change my mind:** Add a dedicated "Identifying variation" subsection in Section 3 with the four figures above. This is a low-effort change and would substantially strengthen the paper's measurement claims.

## Minor concerns

1. **Section 3.1, footnote 4** ("a few districts" with zeroes in unemployment) — give the number of districts and refugees affected.
2. **Section 3.2, eq. (2)** — IPBL is divided by 1000 for "graphical clarity," but this division should be flagged because it changes the unit-of-coefficient interpretation throughout the paper and causes confusion in Section 5 ("a one-unit increase in IPBL represents 1,000 euros"). State explicitly the units in every figure note and every table.
3. **Section 4.1, eq. (3) (main spec)** — the outcome subscript is sometimes $Y_{idts}$ and sometimes $Y_{idt}$; the state subscript $s(t)$ is in the text but disappears from the equation. Clean up.
4. **Section 4.2.2** — the "upper bound" argument for IPBL bias on relocation is informal and not quantified. Give a concrete back-of-the-envelope that bounds the size of the political-sentiment bias, ideally using the FPÖ control in Section 5.1 (`5a_mechanisms.tex`).
5. **Section 5.1, footnote 6** (analysis on wage income is conditional on employment) — mention the size of the selection problem. With employment going from 11% to ~15% under a standard IVUR shift, the conditional-on-employment sample shifts by ~35%; the wage null is uninformative without bounding the selection.
6. **Section 5.5 / Table `rob_ivur_ipbl_arrival`** — IPBL at approval *halves* and loses significance once IPBL at arrival is included; IPBL at arrival is itself insignificant. This is one of the most informative robustness checks in the paper but is treated in passing. Either the IPBL employment effect survives or it does not; the paper should commit to an interpretation. If the protection-date IPBL effect is shared with the arrival-date IPBL, the identification claim that IPBL captures the variation from policy reforms (rather than from broader state-level trends) is weaker than the paper says.
7. **Table 1 (`descriptives_manually_edited.tex`)** — IPBL is reported as €827 (CR) and €519 (subsidiary), but the main text uses IPBL in units of 1000-euro and uses "300 euro" as the standard shift. Make the table units consistent with the regression specification.
8. **`5a_mechanisms.tex` line 11** — "labor demand in these occupations might be correlated with labor demand in other occupations. In this case, the estimates would be upward-biased." The footnote then reports the estimated coefficient for non-desired-occupation IVUR as 0.234 — but this number doesn't appear in any table or figure. Either put it in a table or remove the precision.
9. **Section 5.4 (interaction)** — Table `tab:interactions` reports demeaned IVUR and IPBL but does not report the demeaning means. The "fix IVUR at zero" first row therefore corresponds to a specific (mean-IVUR, mean-IPBL) point that is not clearly labeled.
10. **`5_results.tex` footnote 12** — the elasticity calculation has a typo: "$3.3/11.8$" should be "$3.4/11.8$" given the preceding $0.01/0.29 \approx 3.4\%$.
11. **The "outcomes in month 12" anchor.** The 12-month horizon is the headline throughout (Tables 4, 5; main figure captions). Why 12? Some refugees are still in PES integration programs at month 12. A 18- or 24-month anchor might better capture "post-program" outcomes. Justify the choice.
12. **`Bibliography_base.bib` is a symlink to `bib_andreas.bib`** but `0_master.tex` line 198 loads three .bib files (`bibliography, bibliography2, bib_andreas`). I cannot tell what is actually cited where. Consolidate.
13. **Figure 4 (`fig:descriptive_outcomes_main`)** — the descriptive employment figure is split by gender, but Section 3.4 says nothing about why employment is presented by gender at this point in the paper. The reader infers it; make the connection explicit (since gender heterogeneity is the basis for Section 5.6).

## Methods-referee sanity checks (reduced-form paper type)

- **Identifying assumption stated cleanly** — **partial.** The cell-assignment story (arrival-group FE) is clearly stated and tested via the balance regression in Table 2. The treatment-exogeneity story (district FE + month-year FE absorb regional confounders) is asserted but not directly tested. There is no within-arrival-group event-study style test of pre-trends for the IPBL reforms, even though the data would support one.

- **Treatment definition matches data construction** — **✗.** The municipality-vs-district inconsistency in `3_data.tex` lines 16 vs. 22, the unspecified "open positions" stock-vs-flow choice, and the gap between "potential" and "received" benefits (Section 7, lines 12) all mean the treatment-as-defined and the treatment-as-constructed do not fully align. This is MC2/MC3 territory.

- **Sample inclusion/exclusion documented end-to-end** — **✗.** No flowchart, no per-step counts, BMI/BFA universe comparison is commented out (MC1).

- **Standard errors match design (clustering at randomization unit)** — **partial.** State-year-of-protection is one defensible choice but is not the level of the treatment variation. The paper's preference for state-year over wild-cluster bootstrap (which would be the small-N-clusters defensible inference) is concerning (MC4).

- **Functional form choices justified** — **partial.** The linear-in-IVUR and linear-in-IPBL assumption is consequential — at very high IVUR (>1) or very low IPBL (<400 euro), the linearity is unlikely to hold. The "counterfactual at Viennese benefit levels" simulation in Section 7 sits at the bottom of the IPBL distribution and the linearity assumption is doing a lot of work there.

- **Multiple-testing concerns surfaced where relevant** — **✗.** The paper estimates dynamic effects (60 monthly horizons) × multiple outcomes (8+) × multiple subsamples (gender, protection type, reform-window, etc.). The plots show 95% pointwise CIs; there is no uniform inference correction or sup-t band. With this much testing, some "fading after 30 months" claims will be sensitive.

- **Effect sizes economically meaningful (not just statistically significant)** — **✓.** The 4.2pp employment effect at a baseline of 11.3% is genuinely large (38% in relative terms). The 1pp staying effect for a €100 benefit change is comparable to Ferwerda and is reasonable.

- **Robustness checks address the obvious threats** — **partial.** The robustness section covers: extended sample, reception-facility-only sample, asylum-process > 1 month, exclusion near reform dates, exclusion of pre-protection movers, leave-one-state/nationality, alternative time FE, alternative SE clusters. What is missing: (a) alternative spatial aggregation of IVUR (commuting zone vs. district); (b) alternative timing window for IVUR ($\{-2,-1,0\}$ vs. $\{0,1,2\}$); (c) alternative IPBL including housing; (d) pre-trends test for IPBL reforms; (e) stayers-only specification to address MC5; (f) Oster-style bounds on the IPBL effect for political-sentiment confounding (the FPÖ control is a single robustness, not a bound).

Overall verdict on sanity checks: too many "partial" and "✗" for a ReStud manuscript at this stage. None is fatal individually; collectively they require a major revision.
